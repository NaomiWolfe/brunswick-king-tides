🌊 Why the Tide Table Isn't Enough

Oct 7 to Nov 6, 2026 • St. Simons Village Pier (BRNG1)
Tide predictions do not include wind, rain, or storms. Follow official alerts.

In October 2026, water reached flood stage on 8 high tides. The tide predictions put none of them at flood stage.

Tide predictions only account for the pull of the moon and the sun. On our flat coast, steady northeast winds push ocean water onshore and hold it in the salt marsh. When heavy rain falls at the same time, water drains more slowly. In this period, measured high water at St. Simons ran up to 1.8 feet above the prediction.

🛡️ Stay Informed & Plan Ahead

Preparation tips

Never drive or walk through floodwater. Turn around.

Just 6 inches of fast-moving water can knock you down, and 1 foot of moving water can sweep a vehicle away. Salt water can also badly damage a car's brakes and electrical system.

Plan ahead before king tides:

  • Move vehicles: If your street or lot tends to flood, park on higher ground before the peak tide.
  • Allow extra time: Plan trips around the high tide window when you can.
  • Check alerts that morning: Look for NWS Coastal Flood Advisories and Warnings.
  • Secure outdoor items: Move trash bins and loose items away from low spots.

📖 About the Data & Coastal Datums

Community education project

MHHW (Mean Higher High Water)

The average height of the higher of each day's two high tides. A tide above 0.0 ft MHHW is higher than a typical daily high tide.

MLLW (Mean Lower Low Water)

The average height of the lower of each day's two low tides. Nautical charts and published tide tables measure up from this line.

What is a "king tide"?

On this site, a king tide is a high tide predicted at 1.0 foot or more above MHHW at the Brunswick or St. Simons station. This is a practical guideline, not an official definition.

Tidal epochs & sea level

These datums use the 1983 to 2001 National Tidal Datum Epoch. Local sea level has risen since then, so the same tide reaches higher today.

Predictions come from NOAA CO-OPS stations 8677406 (Howe Street Pier, Brunswick) and 8677344 (St. Simons Island), saved Nov 6, 2026. The Brunswick and St. Simons stations use their own datums, so comparisons between them are approximate. Live readings come from the NOAA National Water Prediction Service gauge BRNG1. The saved data files are in the data folder of this project.
